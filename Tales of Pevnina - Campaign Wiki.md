# Tales of Pevnina: Campaign Wiki

The single reference file for the *Tales of Pevnina* campaign, kept from Wheezy's side of the table. It brings together Joachim's three notebooks, the corrections on them, and everything public on the party's LegendKeeper wiki. Use it to look things up, compare sources, and test theories.

**Ground rules for this file**

- Everything here comes from the notebooks, the corrections file or the LegendKeeper wiki. Nothing is taken from the internet or from published D&D material.
- Every statement points to a source (see the legend below).
- When something is not in the sources, the file says so with the phrase **"Not in the sources."** Absence in this file means the information is not in the notes or on the wiki, not that it is false.
- Theories are always marked *Theory* and kept apart from facts. Section 10 collects them.

## Part 1: Oakhurst and the Sunless Citadel

### Session 1: Adventures in Oakhurst  `[S1]`

*16th of Kythorn, four days before the Summer Solstice.*

- Timon and Draewnin run into twig blights on the road, sticks that fire darts.
- **Oakhurst**: a harbour town known for lumber, orchards and agriculture, trading with bigger cities. Tavern, mayor's house, the Hucrele family house, a statue of a squirrel.
- Remus's grandfather, also called Remus Nutsworth, was a Sentinel of Dwahl and a paladin of Shae `[Corr]`. He slew the vampire Gulthias by trapping him in the tree that Belak later used. Note to self: learn more about the Sciurian people and the history of Oakhurst.
- Wheezy meets **Kalazmir** in the tavern. A minotaur from the Endless Plains (Savage Frontier), nomadic, searching for a cure for his sick elder. Very loyal to his tribe, not afraid of experiments. Mostly the smash guy, but really nice. Wheezy sees him as the leader of the gang.

**The story of the apple**
- For 12 years goblins have surfaced to present a magic red apple. It heals the sick and gives a longer life (Edmund Wildcock ate one). There is also a white apple, which is poisonous.
- Rumours: goblins, a disease-carrying apple, blue fever.
- This year the goblins did not come up. Oakhurst sent a party into the goblin lair: Sir Braford (paladin) and the Hucrele siblings Sharwyn (wizard), Talgen (fighter) and Karakas (ranger). They have not returned.
- The Hucrele family gives the party climbing gear and a healing potion.

**The Sunless Citadel**
- A temple more than a thousand years old, belonging to a dragon cult that worshipped Tiamat, Queen of Dragons. It also has a keep. It sank to the bottom of a ravine after the final clash between Tiamat and Bahamut on the Ashen Plain, in which Tiamat was defeated by Bahamut `[Corr]`. Tiamat is also called the Bane of Bahamut.
- The party descends on ropes past goblin writings. Big trap before the main hall. Two doors: the Dragon Door and the door to kobold territory ("Here, there be dragons").
- Rooms with mystical fountains:
  - Dragon head fountain 1: *Ustagfurey Stufuvog*, "let there be fire". A golden liquid pours out. This turns out to be a dragon breath potion (command word *Ixen Trekis*).
  - Dragon head fountain 2: *Narkazovu*, "let there be dead". A poisonous gas appears. Don't do this again.
  - A door inscribed "Rebuke the dead, open the way".
- They free **Erky Timbers**, a gnome acolyte of Pelor locked in a goblin cell. He says the twig blights help the goblins, and that Belak lives in the Twilight Grove under the citadel, where the Gulthias tree stands.

**Kobold times**
- **Meepo** takes them to the kobold leader, **Priestess Yusdrayl**. The kobolds are in a turf war with the goblins, who stole their white dragon **Calcryx**. The party agrees to protect the kobolds and get Calcryx back.
- The kobolds speak of an evil below: a phytomancer called **Belak**, a dark druid working with vines and mushrooms. "That man stole Calcryx!" He grows the food and the apple; what he touches gets "changed".
- Fight with the goblins. Kalazmir smashes them. **Draewnin is killed** in the battle. Killing blow: not recorded in the notes.

### Session 2  `[S2]`

- Back to kobold territory while securing the area. Deal with **Balsag**, the goblin chief, after he saved Remus. He leads the surviving goblins into the Underdark.
- They lie to **Gnashka**, the kobold envoy meant to bring peace offerings to the goblins.
- The hobgoblin leader **Durnn** has a red hand painted on his armour and wears the Hucrele family signet ring. A shaman hobgoblin wore the family amulet and splint armour, probably Talgen Hucrele's.
- **Mourning Draewnin.** They bury him on the surface. He was an elf of the Arysel'ndor tribe (a kind of jungle elf), who wish to be burned. When fire touched his body, crows and ravens poured down, a woman came down and kissed him, and purple mist rose.
- **Draewnin returns**, brought back by the Raven Queen (guardian of people who suffer, the Shadowfell, goddess of death). He does not feel complete: "self is not self, never whole again". He has a new calling and insists on destroying the tree; to him it was never about balance. His wife **Gnotchmachkaka** was a champion of the Raven Queen and she decided he could live again. Everything about him is darker now: skin deathly grey, hair black, eyes silver.
- Down into Belak's territory. A draconic library with interesting scrolls and a tome on draconic history.
- **Clash with Belak** and his minions: twig blights, Sir Braford and the Hucrele girl. The party wins. Killing blow: not recorded in the notes (Wheezy's own tally for the vine battle on that page: 1 kill). "Johnny" (?) is saved from the tree because he ate half of the first apple. Sir Braford is killed; the Hucrele girl is taken to Oakhurst.
- Miscommunication with Remus about why Wheezy was hoarding the last pieces of the apple. The whole apple is split: a quarter for Kalazmir, three seeds for Wheezy, one seed for Timon.
- The notebook date 23/02/2020 appears on this part, next to "Gulthias tree, vampire" and "Draewnin is back".

### Session 3  `[S3]`

- The **Gulthias tree is burned** down and the root of the evil destroyed.
- Because the tree vanished, everyone who had survived thanks to the apples or had eaten them to heal later died: their connection to it ended with the tree `[Corr]`.
- On the way back Timon and Wheezy find a small library. Opening *Treasures of the Firelord* blasts them away. Timon hears voices: "Thank you for destroying him." One level of exhaustion.
- Loot: tooth and bone of the vampire Gulthias, a branch of the bad tree (Remus judged it), the sword Shatterspike (identified), a wand of entanglement, two unknown scrolls.
- **Timon Diamondback** properly introduced: a big tortle with mushrooms growing on his shell and a shield that looks like a surfboard. Nature and balance vibe, possibly something to do with necrotic mushrooms. He spreads spores wherever he goes.
- **Myconids** (mushroom people), right up Timon's alley. Sentient beings connected to each other, enslaved by Belak for 13 years. Now they want to start a colony.
- On the way up: slay a demon dog, grab gold from the pile (silver necklace, gold necklace). Remus leaves Calcryx's door open.
- Back to the Hucrele family. The girl is not looking good. They take her to the **temple of Pelor** and hand her to **Brother Falkas**, the healer. Hands are the symbol of the Sentinels.
- *18th of Kythorn, two days before the Summer Solstice.* The Hucrele girl is **Sharwyn**; she was studying to go to Lore.
- That evening, the burial of **Sir Braford**, Sentinel of Dwahl. The party will be thanked at the festival next day and named honorary citizens.
- *19th of Kythorn.* **Back to the temple** with the key to the Dragon Door. Entering activates a system that closes the main door, so they must be quick.
  - Room 1: a dragon head with blue flames and a riddle: "We come at night without being fetched, disappear at day without being stolen." Answer: stars.
  - Room 2: five statues whose eyes light up; they animate and slowly re-form. A door with two dragons and two horns opens only with dragon breath (the vial is used up on it).
  - The session ends staring down a hallway at a tomb: *"A dragon priest, entombed alive for transgressions of the law, still retains the honor of his position."*

# 6. Lore

## 6.1 Summary of the lore

*Sources: `LK: Wondragoth Wheezy Mandragora` (City of Lore tab), `LK: Tome of Draconic History`, `LK: Books and Lore`.*

- **City of Lore**: a floating city of scholars. Ranks: Arch-Scribe (the eight heads of the schools of magic), Loremaster, Concordant, Neophyte (sworn to the Oath of the Vellum), Aspirant (student), and the Vellumborn or "Hollowed": oath-breakers turned into milky-eyed, emotionless scribes and workers.
- **Oath of the Vellum**: "I bind myself to the Eternal Quill, that knowledge be my beacon, not my dominion..." Breaking it slowly turns a scholar into a Vellumborn.
- **The eight Arch-Scribes**: Borius Icthylius Mandragora (Abjuration, copper dragonborn), Veyla Thornweave (Conjuration, half-elf), Erasmus Vale (Divination, human), Damaia Vess (Enchantment, tiefling), Gremor Anarkhaos (Evocation, dwarf), Selendra Morvain (Illusion, drow), Malthas Crowborne (Necromancy, kenku), Ophala Wynriver (Transmutation, gnome).
- **The Progenitor dragons**: golden Siberys (magic), gentle Io (life) and cruel Khyber (secret knowledge). Siberys and Khyber fought over the Draconic Prophecy; Siberys was destroyed, creating the Ring of Siberys, and Io bound Khyber beneath the world. Dragonshards are pieces of them: Khyber shards (dark blue or black, deep underground), Io shards or bloodstones (pink, in egg-shaped stones in soil or swamp), Siberys shards (golden, fallen from the ring).
- **The Draconic Prophecy**: read in the moons, stars and the Ring of Siberys. Only dragons, some elves and rakshasa can follow it. It points to the transformation of the world: an end, or a new beginning.
- **Bahamut and Tiamat**: two halves of an older god, Authority, split during the war with the Giant Empire of Ostoria into Honor (Bahamut) and Dominion (Tiamat). Bahamut made the five Metallic dragons, Tiamat the five Chromatic ones.
- **The Battle of Khalas Chasm**: Bahamut chased Tiamat across the planes (the Ten Days' Flight) and beat her above the Styx. Asmodeus offered her sanctuary in Avernus in exchange for her essence; she accepted and is now trapped in the Nine Hells.
- **The Rite of Rebirth**: Bahamut's rite. A called humanoid gives up their possessions, meditates a day and a night, sleeps in an egg-shaped chamber and emerges as a platinum-scaled dragonborn, a champion against Tiamat.
- **The first Chromatic dragons**: Garyx the Red (Harbinger of Wrath), Albioris the White (Hunt), Malaestor the Blue (Force; frozen in Avernus after trying to free Tiamat), Severdin the Green (Secrets; rumoured dracolich), Null the Black (Cruelty).
- **The first Metallic dragons**: Aurinax the Gold (Kings), Lendys the Silver (Justice), Khalkhon the Bronze (Courage; died in the Arcane Age, hoard rumoured underwater), Hlal the Copper (Luck; patron of scholars and gamblers), Tamara the Brass (Mercy; rules the desert city Dhi'dir as an elven woman).
- **Books and Lore index on the wiki**: *Genesis Arcanum* (the Sorcerers of Qruille, their crystal and the collection of life-giving force), *Bodies of the Sky* by A. Herwenys (Siberys, the stars, dragonshards, planar travel), *Rituals and Rites, a historical study* (how rituals were made and why they demand less of casters), the *Tome of Draconic History* and the *Diary of Nurklenak*.

## 6.2 The Dragonscale Sorcerers and the Arcanum Age

*Sources: `V2: Blingdenstone`, `V2: The Merchant's caravan`; `[Corr]` stresses that this lore is very important.*

- Six sorcerer-kings, the Dragonscale Sorcerers, made themselves immortal by harvesting crystals and souls of dragons ("rejuvenation" myths). They were among the first casters of the Arcanum Age, about 1000 years ago.

## 6.5 Tome of Draconic History

*Source: `LK: Tome of Draconic History` (full text from the wiki).* 

#### Draconic creation

The wisest dragons contend that the world was born in battle. According to myth, the emptiness before creation was the domain of three mighty dragons. Golden Siberys was the source of all magic. Gentle Io was the fountain of life. Cruel Khyber was the master of secret knowledge, and of the powers that lurk in the darkness. Together, they held dominion over the fate of all, and they pondered the proper shape of the universe. These three are called the Progenitor dragons, and in their pondering they discovered the Draconic Prophecy, a record of things to come that has been playing out since creation.

Then Siberys and Khyber began to fight over it, destroying Siberys (and creating the Ring of Siberys). Io then used her own body to bind and trap Khyber in the depths of the world, and at the same time restore life to it. Siberys is now (or was) referred to as "the Dragon Above," Io "the Dragon Between" and Khyber "the Dragon Below."

##### Draconic Prophecy

Dragons of old observe and record everything from the position of the moons and stars, to the position of the Ring of Siberys, all of which they study looking for portents and omens of things to come. The prophecy encompasses many many volumes and is said to be as complex and unfathomable as the dragons themselves. A few among the scholarly study snippets of the massive work, but only the dragons with their incredibly long lifespan, some elves, and the immortal rakshasa have the ability and patience to see the prophecy for what it truly is.

It is unknown what the draconic prophecy's nature is. Some argue it is a revelation of the end of the world, others of a new beginning. Whatever the means it is obvious that the prophecy points towards the transformation of the world as we know it.

##### Dragonshards

In the clash between the progenitor dragons, pieces of their essence were ripped off and created so called Dragonshards in three varieties, Khyber shards, Siberys shards and Io shards.

Khyber dragonshards are generally a dark blue or black and can be found beneath the surface of Io in deep underground caverns.

Io dragonshards, also known as bloodstones, are typically pink in color and can be found within egg-shaped stones buried beneath top-soil, or the watery muck in swampy regions.

Siberys dragonshards are golden in color and fall from the Ring of Siberys, which means they are usually found on the surface along the path of the ring

These shards are often mentioned in arcane rituals and are said to be sources of raw power.

#### Bahamut and Tiamat

During the time of the Founding, gods warred amongst themselves and with the original Primordials. Among those created when the True Creator split was a single god more powerful than the rest; its true name has been lost to time, but this god's Intent was Authority and its form appeared draconic. Authority's strength served as a guiding light during the founding days of the Prime Material Plane.

As the Divinium wore on and mortal races began to express their own understandings of the gods, Authority found itself worshipped in two distinct manners; for one, as an ideal to be upheld, a standard of rulership based in justice and protection of the weak. Equally prevalent, however, were those who took absolute control--tyrants, despots, those who rule by force and deception. Despite the sharp difference between these forms, Authority found itself unable to dispute either one, and the god's unity began to slowly split into two halves. Eventually, during the war with the Giant Empire of Ostoria, Authority split entirely into the two draconic gods--Bahamut took up Honor and Tiamat became Dominion. Though diametrically opposed from the start, the two gods had little chance to fight each other; the threat of Giants still loomed large. As a result, the two created protectors of the mortals in their own image: Bahamut created the first five Dragons Metallic as protectors of the good, while Tiamat birthed the five Dragons Chromatic as agents of conquest. When the giants were eventually defeated, pushed back beyond the mountains to the north, the dragons remained.

Being the manifestation of Honor itself, Bahamut (also known as the Platinum Dragon, Scales of Justice, or Father of Dragons), represents justice, protection, and law. Every paladin who upholds their oath is, at the very least, respected, as is any ruler who leads his people with fairness and respect. Those who worship the draconic pantheon as a shining example of good see Bahamut as the ultimate ideal; some believe that every metallic dragon in the pantheon is merely an avatar of the Platinum Dragon. Still others believe him to be an unbending god who cares only for the letter of an oath, rather than the spirit or motivation.

As the manifestation of Dominion, Tiamat (also known as the Mother of Dragons, The Queen of Desire, or The Avaricious One) represents the other end of authority: control, domination, and a desire to obtain. Tyrants often uphold this aspect of Authority to the extreme, obtaining power for themselves and justifying it as the right of conquest. Power belongs to those strong enough to wield it, and Tiamat represents the most powerful entity there is.

##### Rituals of Bahamut

Most of the quests that Bahamut's followers go on are apt to involve opposing Tiamat in some way. They have few formal rituals. Instead of praying aloud, they count their deeds as prayers, supporting goodness and opposing evil. Bahamut cares little for mere words.

###### The Rite of Rebirth

Rarely, humans, elves, halflings, or other humanoid races may hear a call, like a faint question in their hearts, asking them if they want to devote themselves completely to Bahamut. Normally it is first heard before adolescence, but sometimes adults hear it as well. Not all those who are called answer, but those who do may undergo the Rite of Rebirth. Those who commit to this demanding ritual put aside all their weapons and equipment, dressing in a simple linen shift. They meditate for a full day and night, their heads filled with reminders of all they are giving up. If they elect to go on, they then enter an egg-shaped chamber at dawn and sleep until dawn the next day, emerging as a dragonborn, a noble, draconic, platinum-scaled version of their previous shape, ready to become a permanent champion against Tiamat and her spawn.

#### Tiamat

Tiamat was a unique chromatic dragon, who had one head for each primary color of the most common species of chromatics (black, blue, green, red, white). Each head was able to operate entirely independently of each other and had the powers of a member of the respective race of dragonkind. Her body also had traits in common with a wyvern, including a long tail tipped with a poisonous stinger.

##### Mythology

Since Authority split in half and the giant wars ended, these two have been caught in an endless conflict; dragon against dragon, protection against domination. Dragons clashed in the early days, but often as not these conflicts were between the mortal followers of the gods, warring amongst themselves.

Then came the Eternal War. When the self-proclaimed "Gods of the Light" secretly attacked the so-called Dark Ones--Asmodeus, Tiamat, and Tharizdun, among others, who were amassing power for their own ends--Bahamut surprised them by coming to Asmodeus' aid. The assault, after all, represented dishonor and deception. This battle, known to the few who remember it as Honor's Folly, gave rise to an unprecedented sight: angels and Metallic Dragon fighting alongside archdevils to protect the Nine Hells from the largest collective celestial assault in history.

But as the war dragged the entire pantheon into conflict and the Dark Ones demonstrated their vicious, destructive tactics, Bahamut sided with the Gods of the Light. The Dragons Metallic and Dragons Chromatic once more battled in earnest.

During one of countless battles on the Prime Material Plane, Bahamut's forces miraculously broke the ranks of Tiamat's army, and the Mother of Dragons was forced to flee. Bahamut alone pursued her across the Outer Planes in a mad chase known as the Ten Days' Flight, finally engaging her above the River Styx on the slopes of Gehenna. The Battle of Khalas Chasm saw both gods heavily wounded as their physical forms clashed for the first and only time; their fight split the rock, destroying much of the plane and creating new paths for the River Styx to flow through. It is said that by the tenth hour of unbroken battle, Bahamut finally managed a devastating blow, driving Tiamat into the ground, wounded. But as Bahamut raised a claw to land a final, mortal blow, time around them froze. A horned figure in deep red robes--Asmodeus, Lord of the Nine Hells--appeared, offering a deal to the Queen of Desire: unfettered entry for Tiamat and any chromatic dragons into the circle of Avernus within the Nine Hells, and sanctuary within. In return, he wanted an alliance, and portion Tiamat's own magical essence would be used to fuel the devils in the Blood War. Left with no option, Tiamat took the deal, whisked from Bahamut's grasp to safety in Avernus. Bahamut, in disbelief, could only watch as Asmodeus murmured an apology and disappeared into shadow.

In this act, Asmodeus gained a new source of power for the Blood War, but shattered what remained of his friendship with Bahamut. Tiamat, for her part, found that while she was allowed entry into Avernus, Asmodeus had made no promises about getting out. And so, though alive, she found herself trapped. Bahamut, for his part, was left without a quarry. The chromatic dragons were left with their leader trapped in the Hells--while many still took her orders, still others saw Tiamat's imprisonment as a chance to claim power for themselves.

## 8.2 Luck Points

*Source: `LK: Luck Points` (full text from the wiki).* 

#### Luck Points

Replacing 5e Inspiration mechanic. Source: Tales of the Valiant

Summary

- Gain Luck point when you fail save or attack roll
- Gain Luck at the whim of the DM
- Max 5 Luck, roll d4 when receiving a 6th, result is new total
- Spend Luck points to add to any d20 roll total
- Spend 3 Luck points to reroll a d20
Every PC has a special resource called Luck that can be used to influence the result of any of your checks: ability check, attack roll, or save. This feature replaces Inspiration

##### Gaining luck points

When you create your character, start with 0 Luck points.

Gain Luck points in the following ways:

- Once per turn, when you fail an attack roll or save, gain 1 Luck point.
- The GM can award 1 Luck point as a reward for a clever idea, excellent roleplaying, or pursuing an interesting, rather than optimal, choice.
- The GM can award Luck to a party for surviving difficult encounters or achieving story goals (in addition to XP).

##### Losing luck points

You can have a maximum of 5 Luck points at one time. If a PC has 5 Luck points and would gain a 6th point, you must immediately roll a d4 and reset your Luck points to the die result.

##### Spending luck points

You spend Luck points to add to any d20 roll you make.

For example, if you have 4 Luck points, and roll a 13 on the die, you can spend 2 Luck points to make your roll result a 15 (leaving you with 2 Luck points for later).

Alternatively, immediately after you make a check (attack, ability check, or save), you can spend 3 Luck points to reroll a d20.

In either case, you spend Luck after you roll but before the GM declares whether the roll succeeds or fails. Luck can’t offset effects of a natural 1 or create a natural 20.

Luck can't stop a critical miss on a 1 or create a critical hit by adding up to 20. Note that Luck gained from a failed attack or save can't be used to enhance the same roll that generated the Luck - you only accrue Luck after you officially fail the roll!

- Advanced: 1d12 poison damage + poisoned condition + unconscious for 1 minute

Mild: a creature subject to this poison must succeed on a Constitution saving throw or take 1d6 poison damage.

Moderate: a creature subject to this poison must succeed on a Constitution saving throw or take 1d8 poison damage and be Poisoned until the end of your next turn.

Potent: a creature subject to this poison must succeed on a Constitution saving throw or take 1d10 poison damage and be Poisoned for 1 minute.

Deadly: a creature subject to this poison must succeed on a Constitution saving throw or take 2d10 poison damage and be Poisoned for 1 minute. The poisoned creature is also Paralyzed.

Poison type and craft DC modifier

- Injury: DC +10

Contact: Contact poison can be smeared on an object and remains potent until it is Touched or washed off. A creature that touches contact poison with exposed skin suffers its Effects.

Ingested: A creature must swallow an entire dose of ingested poison to suffer its Effects. The dose can be delivered in food or a liquid. You may decide that a partial dose has a reduced Effect, such as allowing advantage on the saving throw or dealing only half damage on a failed save.

Inhaled: These poisons are powders or gases that take Effect when inhaled. Blowing the powder or releasing the gas Subjects Creatures in a 5-foot cube to its Effect. ###### Rebounds and refinements

When you fail or succeed your first attempt you can choose to respectively rebound or refine

In either case you lose components.

Rebound: you lose half the minimum components (min. DC/4) and get another attempt.

Refine: you choose to refine the poison to a higher tier but risk losing it. You use half the number of components of the achieved threshold (threshold DC/4) and make another crafting check, this time you have advantage on your poison brewing roll.

#### Unarmed Strike

PHB'24 p377

Instead of using a weapon to make a melee attack, you can use a punch, kick, head-butt, or similar forceful blow. In game terms, this is an Unarmed Strike, a melee attack that involves you using your body to damage, grapple, or shove a target within 5 feet of you.

Whenever you use your Unarmed Strike, choose one of the following options for its effect.

Damage. You make an attack roll against the target. Your bonus to the roll equals your Strength modifier plus your Proficiency Bonus. On a hit, the target takes Bludgeoning damage equal to 1 plus your Strength modifier.

Grapple. The target must succeed on a Strength or Dexterity saving throw (it chooses which), or it has the Grappled condition. The DC for the saving throw and any escape attempts equals 8 plus your Strength modifier and Proficiency Bonus. This grapple is possible only if the target is no more than one size larger than you and if you have a hand free to grab it.

Shove. The target must succeed on a Strength or Dexterity saving throw (it chooses which), or you either push it 5 feet away or cause it to have the Prone condition. The DC for the saving throw equals 8 plus your Strength modifier and Proficiency Bonus. This shove is possible only if the target is no more than one size larger than you.

#### Escape a Grapple

PHB'24 p367

A Grappled creature can use its action to make a Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check against the grapple's escape DC, ending the condition on itself on a success. The condition also ends if the grappler has the Incapacitated condition or if the distance between the Grappled target and the grappler exceeds the grapple's range.

#### Dying condition

When a creature drops to 0 hitpoints it enters the dying condition

- Creature becomes incapacitated
- Creature drops whatever its holding and becomes prone
- Creature's speed becomes 5 ft. and cannot stand up
- Creature automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws
- Attack rolls against the creature have advantage
- Any attack that hits the creature is a critical hit if the attacker is within 5 ft. of the creature
